Films that make you stare at the walls after you're done watching them.
(2-line pitches are research anchors, not personal notes)

Indian Films:
1. Ee.Ma.Yau - A masterful dark comedy about death rituals in Kerala, showing how a son's promise for a grand funeral leads to chaos. It brilliantly balances humor and pathos while examining human nature during grief.

2. Visaranai - A gut-wrenching true story about police brutality and systemic corruption that won multiple national awards. Will make you question the justice system and stay with you for days.

3. Sethu - The film that redefined Vikram's career, about a college ruffian's transformation through love and tragedy. Haunting performance and raw emotional depth rarely seen in Indian cinema.

4. Mahanadhi - Kamal Haasan's heart-wrenching portrayal of a father searching for his children after being wrongfully imprisoned. The film's realistic approach to trauma and loss makes it devastatingly impactful.

5. Anantharam - A mind-bending narrative experiment by Adoor Gopalakrishnan that tells three different versions of the same story. Will make you question reality and perception.

6. Pithamagan - Vikram delivers one of Indian cinema's most powerful performances as a socially isolated graveyard worker who forms an unlikely friendship. The raw emotional intensity and unconventional storytelling make it unforgettable.

7. Kadhal Koden - A realistic take on young love and social barriers that broke new ground in Tamil cinema. The devastating finale and naturalistic performances make it a landmark in relationship dramas.

8. Lucia - An innovative psychological thriller about dreams and reality that was India's first crowdfunded film. Its narrative structure and twist ending will leave you questioning everything you watched.

9. Sairat - A powerful commentary on caste and honor killing disguised as a romantic drama. The tonal shift in the second half hits like a truck and stays with you forever.

10. Fandry - A devastating look at caste discrimination through the eyes of a young boy falling in love. Its raw authenticity and powerful ending sequence will leave you shaken.

11. Woh Chokri - A haunting drama about adolescence and social inequality with brilliant performances. Its honest portrayal of harsh realities makes it deeply impactful.

Korean Films:
1. The Wailing - A masterful blend of horror, mystery and Korean shamanism that keeps you guessing until the end. The atmosphere of dread and ambiguous ending will haunt you for weeks.

2. Burning - A slow-burn psychological thriller based on Murakami's work that explores class divide and obsession. The ambiguous storytelling and Lee Chang-dong's direction make it a masterpiece of tension.

3. Memories of Murder - Bong Joon-ho's masterpiece about Korea's first serial killer investigation that shows the toll of unsolved cases. Based on true events, it's both a great thriller and a commentary on society.

4. Mother - A unique thriller about a mother's desperate attempt to prove her mentally challenged son's innocence. Bong Joon-ho's direction and Kim Hye-ja's performance create an unforgettable study of maternal love.

5. Vertigo - Not the Hitchcock film, but a mesmerizing Korean drama about obsession and art. Its dreamy atmosphere and complex relationships make it a unique exploration of human desire.

Other:
1. Hereditary - The most disturbing family drama disguised as a horror film that will make you feel genuine dread. Toni Collette's performance and the shocking developments make it an instant classic.

2. Irreversible - A controversial French film told in reverse chronology about revenge and fate. Its brutal honesty and innovative structure make it one of the most impactful films ever made.

3. Incendies - A powerful mystery about twins discovering their mother's past in war-torn Middle East. Denis Villeneuve's masterpiece will leave you speechless with its revelations.

4. Heretic (2024) - A thought-provoking exploration of religion and control that challenges conventional beliefs. The antagonist's perspective offers uncomfortable but compelling arguments.

5. Enemy - Jake Gyllenhaal's dual performance in this surreal psychological thriller about doppelgangers will mess with your mind. The ambiguous ending will have you theorizing for days.

6. Sicario - A brutal look at the drug war through the eyes of an idealistic FBI agent. Denis Villeneuve's direction and Roger Deakins' cinematography make this thriller uncomfortably real.
